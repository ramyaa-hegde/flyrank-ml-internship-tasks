Can historical search signals prioritize CTR review opportunities?

A clear, reproducible workflow that uses February signals to rank observed March position-adjusted CTR opportunities and support April human review.

Abstract

This case study asks whether historical aggregate search signals can help content teams prioritize pages for CTR review. I used public-safe, pseudonymized daily performance data from the FlyRank ML Internship dataset, with February features, a March position-adjusted CTR proxy label, and April action scoring. A Random Forest model was evaluated against a transparent February-only heuristic using a grouped pseudonymized-client holdout. On the same held-out clients, the model achieved Precision@20 of 0.900 versus 0.250 for the baseline, with Average Precision of 0.584 versus 0.339 and ROC-AUC of 0.730 versus 0.486. These measured results support a human-review queue for observed CTR opportunities; they do not establish that changing a title, snippet, or page will cause more clicks.

01 · Introduction / Problem

Help content teams decide where to look first

A team cannot manually inspect every visible page. This case study supports a narrower decision: which pseudonymized content items should move to the top of a human CTR-review queue?

The challenge

A low CTR does not automatically indicate a problem. Expected click-through rate varies with search position, so comparisons should be made within a comparable position tier.

The decision supported

Rank observed CTR gaps for human review. The output is prioritization, not an automated recommendation to edit a page.

03 · Methodology

One simple time order: February → March → April

The time order makes the question easier to explain: learn from February, evaluate against an observed March proxy, then create an April decision-support queue.

February

Features

Impressions, average position, engagement signals, observed-day coverage, GA4 availability, and position bucket.

March

Observed proxy label

Whether CTR is below the median CTR of comparable items in the same position bucket.

April

Human-review labels

Review, investigate, or monitor. No site or content change is automated.

Validation and leakage controls. The Random Forest and the February-only baseline are evaluated on the same grouped pseudonymized-client holdout. The final validation has 12 held-out clients and zero client overlap. Identifiers, March outcome fields, future-window fields, product flags, action labels, and target-derived columns are excluded from model inputs.

The transparent baseline ranks February items in positions 4–20 by February impression volume. The model uses median imputation with missingness indicators for numeric fields and one-hot encoding for the February position bucket.

05 · Limitations & Honest Framing

What the result does—and does not—support

What it supports

Observed, position-adjusted ranking of items for human CTR review on held-out pseudonymized clients.

What it does not support

A claim that a title, snippet, or page edit will cause more clicks, or a claim about Google's proprietary ranking algorithm.

  • The March label is a position-adjusted CTR proxy, not direct business impact or user satisfaction.
  • March availability and the eligibility rule are outcome-window choices and must be considered when interpreting the results.
  • Performance may vary across future periods, other clients, query mixes, seasonality, and search-result layouts.
  • Human review remains required for every review or investigation label.
